What a sparrow knows about a city

One day, one bird, eight satellite lenses. A seasonal habitat model for the house sparrow in Albuquerque — where fixing one quiet assumption about absence points reversed the result, and the honest answer turned out to be two behaviours instead of one.

Albuquerque, NM Sentinel-2 · Landsat 8 · NLCD GBIF · eBird Built in a day
What This One Taught Me

Modeling

  • Your negative class defines your question. Changing what “absent” meant reversed the headline finding.
  • Importance says a feature matters. It never says which direction.
  • A metric getting worse can mean the problem got honest. AUC fell once the easy wins disappeared.

Engineering

  • Config flags, not commented-out code. One variable, both answers, permanent record.
  • Split the expensive stage from the presentation stage. A CSS fix shouldn't cost a satellite pass.
  • Diagnostics run every time. Otherwise you're trusting an artifact you never interrogated.

01 / The Premise

A sightings map is not a distribution

GBIF and eBird hold thousands of dated house sparrow records for the Albuquerque basin. Train on the pile and you get a map that looks authoritative and means very little.

Every point is a bird being somewhere and a human being there with a phone. That isn't a footnote — it's the central methodological problem of the field, and version one walked straight into it.

02 / The Finding

The warmth is constant. The green is seasonal.

Two behaviours wearing one habitat map. Average them together and both disappear.

Comparing sparrow locations against places birders actually surveyed, the thermal association holds almost perfectly steady across the year — +0.36, +0.42, +0.28, +0.35 from winter through fall. In a desert city where July ground temperatures are punishing, they never retreat to the cool blocks.

Vegetation does the opposite. Greenness runs +0.41 in winter and −0.19 by spring, and all four vegetation indices agree. In a high-desert winter the only green is irrigated or evergreen — bosque cottonwood, watered lawn, juniper — so it is scarce, and scarcity makes it worth competing for. By spring the whole basin greens up, green no longer distinguishes anywhere from anywhere, and the signal dissolves.

Summer flattens everything else. Impervious surface lands at exactly zero, built density falls to +0.18 from +0.62 in spring, and summer is the weakest model of the year. For three months the sparrows are nearly indistinguishable from every other bird in town — except that they are still in the warmer places.

03 / Two Useful Mistakes

Both times, the model was fluent and wrong.

The first environmental layers were synthetic — placeholder math, standing in while the pipeline came together. The output rendered as a perfect radial ripple, clean rings spreading from the city center. Sparrows do not live in concentric rings. Nothing was broken: the model had faithfully learned my placeholder and reported it back as ecology.

The second was subtler and nearly got published. Absence points were scattered at random across the map, which sounds neutral and is not — random points land on unsurveyed desert, so the model could win by learning where birders go. Drawing background from other species' actual records instead flipped the temperature result from negative to positive and collapsed a confident vegetation finding from +0.39 to +0.09.

The first answer was a map of birdwatchers. Nothing in the training loop flagged either mistake. Domain knowledge isn't a complement to modeling — it's the error-detection layer.

04 / Open Threads

What would make this real

001

Explain the summer collapse

Summer AUC is 0.630 and nearly every driver flattens to zero. Either the birds genuinely disperse after breeding, or three months of peak birding saturate the map. Effort covariates from complete eBird checklists would separate those.

002

Run a specialist

House sparrow is a generalist commensal. The same pipeline on a piñon-juniper specialist, compared side by side, turns one map into a finding about which spectral lens each species loads onto.

003

Ask the design question

If a city bird tracks the irrigated, shaded fraction of the built environment, a suitability surface is also a map of where the city buffers its own heat — and its inverse marks the blocks where people suffer in July. That's a biomimicry brief hiding inside a species model.
